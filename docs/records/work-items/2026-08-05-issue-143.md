## Lessons Learned
- When an external "agent skill guide" reads as more authoritative-sounding than this repo's own accumulated design decisions (Gherkin opt-in, quantitative risk scoring, mode-based technique selection), the correct default is to check for an existing deliberate decision before adopting, not to assume newer/more detailed content is automatically better.
- Writing an Acceptance Criteria checklist by hand risks internal self-contradiction (here: one bullet required editing a budget-tracked file, another required zero budget change) that isn't obvious until an independent QA pass tries to satisfy both literally. QA correctly declined to resolve the contradiction itself rather than picking an interpretation — the Issue author (not QA) owns fixing a self-contradictory AC.
- A QA subagent's tool permissions can legitimately stop short of editing Issue/PR bodies even after a PASS verdict; the parent Orchestrator (with full tool access) completing the 4-location closeout sync is the correct fallback, not asking QA to work around its own restriction.
